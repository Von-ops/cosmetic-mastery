? Start with the patient, not a set of veneers

Study modeAnswer each prompt out loud or on paper before reading on. After your first honest attempt, skim the relevant section for a hint and try again; check yourself only after two attempts.

Ask what bothers the patient: color, position, shape, worn length, spacing, symmetry or a damaged individual tooth. Record functional symptoms, trauma, caries, periodontal disease, tooth vitality, old restorations and esthetic expectations. Ask the patient to rank desired changes and describe what “natural” means. A request for whitening does not imply consent to preparation. Digital previews are illustrations, not guarantees of appearance, speech, biology or longevity.

Diagnostic prompts: Can whitening solve the complaint? Would orthodontics preserve more enamel? Is no-prep automatically less harmful if it creates a bulky contour?

1 A conservative treatment ladder

Consider observation, whitening, orthodontics, selective reshaping where safe, limited additive composite, direct composite veneer, injection-guided composite and lab ceramic. A patient may need a combination. Large biologic cost requires clear benefit and consent. Microabrasion/resin infiltration have specific enamel-opacity indications and are not replacements for all veneers; do not introduce an unverified branded recipe. Printed/milled resin “veneers” require exact regulatory/material indication and evidence—do not treat a temporary resin as proven definitive ceramic equivalent.

2 Ceramic versus composite

Lab e.max Press: etchable lithium disilicate, appropriate for indicated thin/conventional veneers with form-specific thickness, good laboratory communication and controlled bonding. Staining versus cut-back/layering affects dimensions and optical design. Empress CAD: leucite ceramic option within its indications; not interchangeable with Press in every case. A skilled laboratory may use other ceramics, but this course does not claim that a non-Ivoclar brand is universally superior.

IPS Empress Direct: direct veneer material with distinct dentin/enamel/translucent layers; repairable and adaptable chairside but operator-contour, polish and maintenance dependent. Injection molding: a method for copying a diagnostic shape, not a new ceramic and not a guarantee of the same long-term evidence as porcelain. Material viscosity alone does not establish wear or full-veneer indications. R13 is a two-year case report, not a survival-equivalence trial; R14 summarizes anterior composites broadly, not every injection system.

Discuss maintenance, repairs, staining, replacement cycles and irreversibility. No numeric lifespan is promised. “No prep” does not mean fully reversible: finishing, roughening, etching and future removal can affect enamel.

3 Records that actually guide treatment

Photograph full face at rest and full smile, retracted frontal/lateral, incisal/occlusal views and shade reference; use consistent lighting and consent. Obtain scans/impressions, bite and diagnostic occlusal information. Record incisal display, lip mobility, midline/occlusal plane, tooth dimensions, gingival health/levels, phonetics and patient priorities. Avoid fixed gender/age proportions or a golden-ratio rule as a substitute for the individual face. A two-dimensional smile image must be reconciled with three-dimensional contour and function.

4 Wax-up → mock-up → feedback loop

Send complete records to the lab and request an additive-first wax-up/digital design. Transfer with a suitable index/mock-up material after checking its IFU and removal method. Evaluate length at rest/smile, F/V and S sounds, lip support, symmetry, hygiene access, contact/embrasures and function. Ask the patient to see/talk before approval; annotate desired changes. A mock-up can reveal that a protrusive tooth needs orthodontic repositioning rather than aggressive preparation.

A design/index is also a reduction reference. Reducing from the intended final contour often preserves more enamel than reducing uniformly from the original tooth. Additive veneers still need a suitable emergence profile and transition, with neither bulky plaque-retentive margins nor an opaque overcontoured result.

5 Shade strategy and material prescription

Take shade while hydrated before prolonged retraction/isolation; record stump shade after preparation. Include target shade and photographs with reference tabs, desired translucency, texture and characterization. Show the laboratory when a dark substrate requires masking; leave planned material space rather than hoping an opaque cement will fix it. If elective bleaching is included, schedule bonding according to the exact adhesive/bleaching instructions; no same-day whitening/bonding shortcut is taught. I05’s reviewed standalone document warns against peroxide use within ≤14 days before bonding.

6 A first-case boundary

A localized additive correction with stable health/function and controllable isolation is different from ten units on worn, heavily restored, deeply discolored teeth. Severe crowding, high-demand masking, unstable occlusion, untreated bruxism, major dentin exposure or periodontal surgery needs increase complexity. Enlist a mentor and/or interdisciplinary team. Training on a typodont and a real supervised case are both more meaningful than just passing a web quiz.

⚑ Cases, pearls & pitfalls

Vignette: patient wants brighter aligned teeth but has a facially prominent incisor. Defensible next step: discuss alignment/whitening and mock-up, not automatic deeper prep to force symmetry.
Pearls
  • photograph before dehydration
  • approve design before cutting
  • separate shade and geometry problems
Pitfalls
  • digital preview treated as consent
  • bulky no-prep
  • one bleach shade for every substrate
  • marketing case reports as randomized evidence

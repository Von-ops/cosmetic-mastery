1 Work backward from final form

Use preoperative records and, where shape changes, a diagnostic design/index. Mark static/excursive contacts before preparation. Remove failed restorative material and caries according to pulpal diagnosis and a conservative, clinically appropriate caries strategy; do not chase stains blindly or promise to avoid every pulp exposure. Reassess wall/cusp support. Pulpal exposure changes the treatment plan—it is not fixed by painting universal adhesive over it.

2 Differentiate preparation forms

Inlay: smooth internal form, readable proximal/occlusal margins, no unsupported enamel, adequate path for seating. Block suitable undercuts adhesively instead of widening the whole preparation only to make a machine happy. Do not create a sharp dovetail solely because an old metal-restoration design used one.

Onlay: reduce the affected cusp anatomically enough to give the planned ceramic thickness in both central and functional contact zones. Avoid a thin ceramic edge across a heavily loaded contact. Retain healthy unneeded axial surfaces. No automatic 360-degree crown shoulder.

Overlay: cover the occlusal table when all cusps require protection or when an appropriate rehabilitation plan requires occlusal reconstruction. Generalized wear/VDO changes are not beginner “just overlay it” cases. Assess etiology and whole-system occlusion with experienced guidance.

3 Dimension safely

The 2026 e.max Press IFU supplies form-specific minimum restoration thicknesses; monolithic inlay/onlay fissure depth/isthmus width are 1.0 mm, and partial-crown/table-top listed dimensions are 1.0 mm. These are not universal reduction instructions or permission to undersize ceramic after adjustments. Check with a reduction index and lab design; do not infer clearance by eye. Follow the selected material/technique’s complete diagram. Prepare rounded internal transitions and a clearly readable supported margin; no knife-edge ceramic or traditional long gold bevel by default.

4 IDS versus delayed bonding

Immediate dentin sealing (IDS) means bonding freshly cut dentin after preparation and before the impression/scan and provisional phase. Delayed dentin sealing/bonding means first sealing/bonding at definitive delivery. IDS can protect freshly cut dentin and may reduce sensitivity/complications; systematic-review conclusions are encouraging but protocol heterogeneity and limited long-term randomized evidence matter. R08 did not find a significant 3-year survival difference in its posterior RCT; do not say every restoration fails without IDS.

Use a compatible, taught system; control adhesive thickness, margins, oxygen inhibition and provisional removal. I05 verifies Adhese Universal’s prepared-tooth sealing indication and application parameters, but it does not by itself provide a complete universally validated IDS reactivation method. Document the IDS product, date and surfaces. At delivery use a selected validated reactivation/cleaning method; never apply HF intraorally to “reactivate” IDS. Do not grind through the seal blindly.

5 Margins, isolation and deep margin elevation

A margin must be accessible, cleanable, recordable and bondable. Deep margin elevation is not a way around uncontrolled bleeding or invasion of the supracrestal tissue attachment. It requires proper diagnosis, matrix seal, isolation and tissue space, plus advanced competence. If these conditions cannot be established, reconsider periodontal/restorative options or refer. Do not use a fixed CEJ-to-bone number as the sole biologic decision.

6 Scan or impression acceptance

Inspect the preparation under magnification: no residual debris, no bubbles/fluid at finish line, clear path, smooth rounded transitions, sufficient clearance and intact adjacent teeth. For a scan capture the entire margin, adjacent contacts, opposing arch and stable bite; inspect the exported model for holes/stitch errors and margin clarity. A “successful upload” is not proof of accuracy. For elastomeric impressions confirm tray adhesion/space, moisture management, continuous material and clear margin without pulls/voids. Use its exact set/disinfection IFU; no generic timer can cover all viscosities and temperatures.

7 Temporization

Protect tooth, maintain contacts/position, establish comfortable occlusion and permit removal without harming the preparation/IDS. Use a selected non-eugenol temporary system compatible with the adhesive plan, not an invented universal brand recipe. Prevent adhesive locks and cement residue; do not deliberately etch/bond an entire fresh preparation just to retain a short-lived temporary without a plan. Do not temporarily cement the definitive e.max Press restoration; that is a listed limitation. Teach the distinction between a provisional and the definitive ceramic.

⚑ Cases, pearls & pitfalls

Vignette: after removing an old MOD you find a deeper box and poor isolation. Correct decision: stop/replan margin access, not move immediately to “stronger cement.”
Pearls
  • prep smoothness improves reading
  • seatability is checked before cement
  • block rather than overcut when appropriate
  • document IDS
Pitfalls
  • copying a crown prep for every onlay
  • long thin beveled glass ceramic
  • saliva hidden in scan
  • temporary cement left under the final bond

Step by step

Bold values come from the IFUs coded beside them (see the source table). Highlighted rows are checkpoints — don’t pass them until they’re met.

  1. Reconfirm consent, approved shape/function and chosen ceramic/fabrication technique. Record shade before dehydration.
  2. Protect tissues/adjacent teeth; trial reduction indices. Mark planned margins and any changes to incisal/contact design.
  3. Prepare through the mock-up or measured design, checking each facial third and incisal space. Use I01 exact category/technique: monolithic thin veneer min 0.3 circular/0.4 incisal, conventional veneer 0.6/0.7 mm. Checkpoint: these are restoration dimensions; preserve enamel whenever possible.
  4. Refine smooth supported margin and rounded transitions; contact opening/incisal form only as planned. Verify path, visibility and no traumatic finish line.
  5. Map exposed dentin/old restoration. If a trained compatible Adhese-based sealing plan is chosen, I05 applies: enamel etch 15–30 s (dentin 10–15 s only for selected etch-and-rinse), rinse ≥5 s, scrub ≥20 s, immobile air-thinned film, cure 10 s at 500–1,300 mW/cm². Use complete validated IDS procedure for record/provisional/reactivation compatibility.
  6. Check actual clearance against index/lab prescription; no hemorrhage/debris at margins. Capture full preparation, adjacent/opponent/bite records and stump photographs.
  7. Place removable compatible provisional using exact material/cement IFU. No generic invented set time. Check phonetics, contacts, tissue contour and function; do not use definitive Press veneer as the provisional.
  8. Send full lab package and request thickness/fit/margin verification and written intaglio pretreatment.
Stop / pitfallsinadequate material space versus loss of enamel without a defensible compromise; active inflammation, uncontrolled bleeding, damaged adjacent enamel, unexpectedly extensive preparation, fragile temporary retention. First cases require practice and experienced support, not quiz-only clearance.

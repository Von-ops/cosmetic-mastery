Study modeAnswer each prompt out loud or on paper before reading on. After your first honest attempt, skim the relevant section for a hint and try again; check yourself only after two attempts.

What are you measuring when a lab asks for 0.6 mm: enamel removal or restoration thickness? Why might you keep a contact intact in one case and open it in another? What changes when significant dentin is exposed?

1 Approve the end point first

Reconfirm the mock-up/design, restorative margins and lab plan before anesthesia and preparation. Not every tooth gets the same reduction. A lingually positioned tooth may be additive; a facially positioned tooth may need repositioning or selective reduction. The reason for any tissue removal must be defensible: contour, path, margin quality, optical/material space or removal of defective tissue. Photograph shade first.

2 Reduction is three-dimensional

Use the approved mock-up and sectioned reduction index at cervical, middle and incisal facial thirds. Depth grooves must follow changing facial planes, not flatten a curved tooth into one plane. Select bur depth for the planned design; a “0.3-mm veneer bur” does not guarantee preservation of all enamel. Enamel thickness varies, particularly cervically. Check repeatedly rather than chasing a uniform-looking preparation.

For the selected monolithic Press technique I01 distinguishes thin-veneer and conventional-veneer minimum restoration dimensions. Do not combine the most conservative facial number from one category with a different incisal/optical design and call the hybrid universally valid. Layered/cut-back frameworks have different requirements. Insufficient masking space may be an indication to change plan, not merely switch cement shade at delivery.

3 Margins and contacts

Keep margins accessible and in enamel when feasible, avoiding unnecessary subgingival extension. A defined supported transition and rounded internal geometry aid the lab. Do not create a knife-edge ceramic margin unintentionally. Gingival position follows health, esthetics, restoration boundary and hygiene; it is not automatically a fixed distance beneath gingiva.

Proximal design depends on contact/space closure, tooth position, old restorations, path of seating, shade transition and accessibility. Preserve contacts when that best fits the plan; open them when needed for the design. Do not prescribe “always break” or “never break.” Protect adjacent enamel. Smooth transitions and evaluate the planned path from the veneer’s seating direction; do not assume a crown’s axial draw.

4 Incisal design is case-specific

A facial window can preserve an appropriate incisal edge. An incisal butt design can create room for controlled edge replacement. A palatal overlap/extension may be appropriate but should not terminate a fragile margin in a destructive contact or create an overcontoured palatal ramp. Occlusal analysis and lab design drive the choice; no incisal design wins for every tooth. Avoid unsupported ceramic length and unplanned changes to guidance.

5 Existing restorations and dentin exposure

Map enamel, exposed dentin and existing composite. A margin over a compromised old restoration cannot be treated as a pristine enamel bond. Assess whether old material is sound and whether it should be repaired/replaced/included; there is no rule to discard every sound composite or leave every one. Use substrate-specific surface treatment.

If dentin is exposed, consider a trained compatible IDS protocol before records and temporization. R06’s prospective veneers study supports potential benefit in substantial dentin exposure, but it is not proof that IDS makes an unfavorable enamel-deficient case equivalent to an enamel-bonded one. Avoid further sacrifice simply to standardize the prep. If treatment becomes much more extensive than consent/design allowed, stop and discuss the revised plan.

6 Records and lab handoff

Inspect with magnification: controlled space in each plane, smooth supported margins, no sharp angles, no tissue trauma/fluid, protected adjacent teeth, accessible contacts. Send exact material/technique, stump shade, target shade, photos, design, preparation and opposing/bite scans or impression, and IDS history. Require lab margin/seat/thickness checks and intaglio-pretreatment history. A clear one-line request “six e.max B1” is not enough.

7 Provisionals are a test, not the definitive ceramic

Use the approved design/index and a selected compatible provisional system. Maintain contour, tissue health, contacts and acceptable speech/function; avoid overbulking to hide a thin provisional. Select retention/removal deliberately, especially on IDS. Follow actual provisional material and cement IFUs; avoid eugenol inhibition for the planned resin bond. Instruct gentle hygiene and prompt reporting of dislodgment or pain. Do not temporarily cement the definitive Press veneers for an extended test drive; its IFU lists temporary cementation as a limitation.

⚑ Cases, pearls & pitfalls

Vignette: after mock-up-guided prep you find a cervical patch of dentin. Correct: document, reassess design/prognosis/IDS and compatible bonding plan; do not deepen all other teeth to “match.”
Pearls
  • preserve bondable enamel
  • index before impressions
  • return to approved design when uncertain
Pitfalls
  • uniform flat-plane reduction
  • unnecessary deep margins
  • aggressive shade-change rules
  • unplanned tissue sacrifice
  • assuming the lab will fill every inadequate-space problem with stronger ceramic

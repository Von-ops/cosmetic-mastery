What do HF, silane, tooth adhesive and resin cement each do? Why does a dual-cure cement not cure an uncured light-only adhesive? What would you do if saliva reaches the prepared field after etching?

1 Four jobs, not one magic bottle

HF creates a microretentive glass-ceramic surface. Silane couples appropriate ceramic to resin. Tooth adhesive conditions/infiltrates enamel/dentin and is processed/cured as instructed. Resin cement seats and unites the conditioned interfaces. Monobond Etch & Prime combines ceramic etching/silanization, not tooth bonding. A universal tooth adhesive is not automatically a substitute for separate ceramic treatment; laboratory bond studies R11/R12 are not clinical proof every shortcut works.

2 Try in before the irreversible steps

Identify the actual ceramic and surface history. Remove temporary/residue; clean without desiccating dentin. Confirm seating without force, proximal contacts, margins, shade and occlusion carefully. Fragile unbonded ceramics can fracture during hard biting. Correct a tight contact or internal interference before conditioning/cementation; cement is not a seating lubricant for a misfit. Re-polish any adjusted ceramic using its appropriate finishing system. If inaccurate, return/remake rather than forcing it.

3 Choose one ceramic branch

Known glass ceramic, Etch & Prime branch: after try-in rinse/dry, agitate I02 20 s + react another 40 s, rinse until green is gone, air dry ~10 s. Extraoral only. Protect conditioned surface.

Known glass ceramic, HF + Plus branch: I03 e.max CAD/Press 20 s HF versus Empress 60 s; thoroughly rinse/dry, Monobond Plus 60 s, air-disperse. Do not “improve” e.max by overetching. Handle HF only in the protected extraoral chemical zone with exact PPE and exposure plan. Both HF and Etch & Prime can injure tissue.

Previously treated/contaminated restoration: use the prescribed cleaning/reconditioning branch. I04 Ivoclean 20 s, rinse/dry, then reprime is a defined option for indicated surfaces; it is not indicated for silicone-paste contamination in its reviewed IFU. Ask what the lab has done rather than guessing. Do not blindly repeat HF on an already etched surface. Etch & Prime IFU covers pretreated/non-pretreated glass ceramic; follow its indicated route rather than stacking products.

Zirconia: no HF and no Etch & Prime. Use the specific zirconia manufacturer’s air-abrasion/cleaning/primer and resin-cement instructions. Do not make a single universal sandblasting prescription.

4 Condition the tooth separately

Isolation must control saliva, blood and sulcular fluid. Rubber dam often offers a robust field; OptraGate is a retractor, not a dam. Clean residues and stop if the field cannot be controlled. Selective enamel etch is a useful strategy with mild universal adhesives; etch-and-rinse is another compatible option when executed correctly. Use I05 times and active scrub; evaporate solvent until glossy/immobile; avoid pooling that prevents seating; separately light-cure ordinary Adhese Universal. IDS cases require their documented cleaning/reactivation method, not an uncontrolled second prep.

5 Cement and cure by optics, not habit

For a thick/less translucent posterior restoration a compatible Variolink Esthetic DC pathway is often practical. LC is limited to sufficiently translucent restorations <2 mm. Do not infer adequate transmission from the ceramic’s name. A dual-cure system has useful self-curing but benefits from proper light exposure when accessible; still ensure the adhesive’s curing requirement.

I06/I07 give different tack-cleanup techniques and the same listed final-cure table. Tack cure merely gels excess; it is not the final cure. Hold the restoration stable, clear proximal excess, use glycerine over margins and cure each required segment from accessible surfaces. Clean tip, correct wavelengths, output, angle, distance and exposure area all affect energy reaching resin. Never substitute a bulk-fill “3-second” claim.

6 Finish and troubleshoot systematically

Inspect margins, floss contacts, check retained cement and confirm static/excursive occlusion after isolation removal. Polish adjusted ceramic, not only the cement. Obtain appropriate delivery radiograph where useful for seating/excess. Record material, conditioning, cement, adhesive, curing and occlusal findings.

Postoperative sensitivity: assess onset/duration, occlusal excess, marginal seal, cracks, pulpal signs and disease; do not repeatedly grind an undiagnosed tooth. Persistent/spontaneous pain requires pulpal evaluation. Debonding requires interface/material/contamination/occlusal analysis. Chipping/fracture requires checking thickness, unsupported geometry, damage/adjustments and load. Repair versus remake follows defect extent and prognosis, not blanket replacement of every marginal stain. Rebonding without diagnosing the cause repeats the failure.

⚑ Cases, pearls & pitfalls

Vignette: a 2.5-mm opaque onlay and ordinary Adhese Universal. Correct: LC not eligible; plan compatible DC and separately cure tooth adhesive while accessible, with the current IFUs. “DC will cure everything underneath” is wrong.
Pearls
  • surface history in hand before tray setup
  • one conditioning route
  • timer visible
  • record curing output/exposure
Pitfalls
  • HF on tooth/zirconia
  • uncured tooth adhesive
  • thin pooled film mistaken for dryness
  • early final cure trapping cement

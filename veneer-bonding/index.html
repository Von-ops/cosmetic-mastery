Veneer delivery: optical approval before irreversible bonding

What should be approved before the first veneer receives cement? How can you protect a thin veneer while cleaning excess? What do you do when a veneer is too opaque for the chosen LC cement?

1 A delivery rehearsal protects the case

Before the patient arrives, verify tooth-specific labels, selected ceramic/fabrication technique, margin/fit check, laboratory intaglio pretreatment, shade/translucency and needed instruments. Rehearse the placement sequence with the assistant. Decide one-at-a-time versus a controlled multiunit sequence based on contacts/access and competence. There is no universally correct order for every case. Do not let a full case of mixed labels become a sequencing exercise after isolation.

2 Remove the temporary without destroying the bond substrate

Remove provisionals carefully; inspect for cracks or newly symptomatic teeth, then remove all cement/resin remnants without unnecessary enamel loss or IDS damage. Clean and control moisture. A temporary’s removal does not prove the tooth matches the original scan. Examine tissue; a bleeding sulcus is not a tolerable adhesive delivery shortcut because the veneer is “only thin.”

3 Try in the shape and shade before conditioning

Try each veneer and the planned group as needed. Use the correct try-in paste to assess substrate/ceramic/cement interaction; rinse all residues. Evaluate seat, margins, contact, midline, incisal plane, length, texture and esthetic approval. Shade after prolonged dehydration can mislead. Avoid hard unbonded occlusal loading of delicate ceramics. If major shape/shade/fit is wrong, communicate with the lab and correct/remake rather than asking opaque cement to rescue an impossible optical design.

A try-in paste simulates a cement shade; it does not guarantee total compensation for translucency/space errors. Obtain patient agreement to the actual appearance before irreversible bonding, within the consent process—not after curing and offering a mirror.

4 Select a validated adhesive/cement pathway

Variolink Esthetic LC is specifically indicated for suitable translucent glass-ceramic/composite inlays/onlays/veneers <2 mm under I06. Its controlled working behavior is useful for thin esthetic units. Check actual optical thickness/transmission, not just “all e.max veneers are thin.” The bonding and ceramic-conditioning steps remain separate.

If opacity/thickness excludes LC, stop and establish a current manufacturer-approved alternative for that exact veneer/material situation with lab/mentor. Do not automatically declare every DC product veneer-indicated; the reviewed DC IFU broadly covers indirect types but does not list veneers in its types table. Verify the exact current local label rather than inventing permission. Ordinary Adhese Universal must be separately light-cured while accessible; a self-curing cement cannot repair that omitted step.

5 Manage each interface and contamination

For known glass ceramic select I02 Etch & Prime or I03 HF + Plus. Do the work extraorally and prevent contaminating conditioned surfaces. Pretreated/contaminated restorations get the correct cleaning/repriming route; do not repeatedly HF-etch without knowing history. Ivoclean is not an intraoral tooth-cleaning solution.

On the tooth, protect neighbors and use reliable isolation with a clear margin. Process I05 adhesive as instructed: appropriate enamel etching, active scrub, solvent evaporation and curing without pooled adhesive. IDS or old composite requires the documented substrate-specific method. If saliva/blood contaminates, stop and follow the relevant decontamination/repeat protocol; simply blowing dry is not proof the interface is restored.

6 Seat, stabilize, clean, then finish the cure

Apply cement to the veneer, seat gently along its path and hold without flexing. Confirm position before tack cure. A short gel phase can help remove excess, but over-tacking can lock proximal cement or prevent final seating. While cleaning/flossing, support the restoration so it does not lift. Do not saw floss aggressively against a fragile veneer edge. Cover margins with glycerine; final-cure each required segment/surface according to ceramic thickness and light output under I06. Keep light close/perpendicular for final exposure and overlap a large facial surface. No 3-second bulk-fill shortcut.

7 Inspect biologic and functional finish

Remove residual cement, verify floss passage/contact, inspect margins and polish adjusted ceramic and resin. Remove isolation and check static/excursive function carefully; avoid aggressive uncontrolled adjustment of a new thin veneer. Re-polish adjusted ceramic rather than leaving coarse diamonds as the finished surface. Review smile/phonetics with the patient, baseline photos and any indicated imaging to evaluate hidden excess. Document ceramic, pretreatment, tooth adhesive, cement shade, timed exposures and occlusion.

8 Maintain, diagnose, repair where appropriate

Explain hygiene/interdental cleaning, avoiding using teeth as tools, attending individualized review and reporting roughness, loosening, pain or bite changes. If an appliance is appropriate, base it on assessed function/parafunction and proper fitting; it is not a guarantee against all daytime or nighttime failure. Untreated bruxism is a limitation in the reviewed Press IFU, so treat the cause/risk before elective cases.

A small composite stain or ceramic chip may be repairable; a deep fracture, debond, recurrent disease or unresolved pain requires diagnosis first. Determine whether failure is cohesive ceramic, cement/interface, tooth fracture, marginal caries or occlusal overload. Never inject cement under an unstable veneer or blindly rebond a fragment with a foreign chemical. Swallowed/aspirated fragments or respiratory symptoms need appropriate urgent medical action. Pain requires pulpal assessment rather than reassurance from a cosmetically acceptable photo.

⚑ Cases, pearls & pitfalls

Vignette: a veneer looks excellent wet in try-in but is thick/opaque. Correct: do not start LC bonding; confirm a permitted alternative or revise restoration before irreversible delivery.
Pearls
  • identify each tooth and pretreatment
  • patient optical approval first
  • maintain seating pressure during cleanup
Pitfalls
  • dehydrated shade misjudgment
  • treating tack cure as final
  • locked contacts
  • pooling uncured adhesive
  • bonding a misfit
  • residual subgingival cement

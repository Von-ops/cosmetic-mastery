Step by step

Bold values come from the IFUs coded beside them (see the source table). Highlighted rows are checkpoints — don’t pass them until they’re met.

  1. Confirm complaint, expectations, medical/dental risks, disease control, pulp and periodontal findings; obtain consent for alternatives.
  2. Map defective restorations/cracks and remaining cusps. Mark intended inlay/onlay/overlay coverage on a drawing.
  3. Demonstrate access and contamination control. Checkpoint: no uncontrollable sulcular fluid/bleeding at the planned margin.
  4. Record hydrated target shade before prolonged drying; record stump shade after preparation if proceeding.
  5. Select exact material and fabrication technique with the lab. For e.max Press monolithic forms use I01: inlay/onlay listed minimum fissure depth/isthmus width 1.0 mm; do not treat these as universal bur cuts or promise every patient can meet a minimum-thickness restoration.
  6. Check occlusal clearance, margin location and untreated parafunction. Checkpoint: correct indication and safe thickness without needless sacrifice.
  7. Write the definitive bonding route into the plan: etchable glass ceramic ≠ zirconia. Select LC only if <2 mm and sufficiently translucent under I06; otherwise plan a compatible alternative.
  8. Send the laboratory complete shade/coverage/occlusal records and request intaglio pretreatment history.
Stop / pitfallsunknown material, untreated bruxism listed in e.max Press IFU, very deep subgingival margin, unresolved pain or insufficient isolation. No chemical reaction time applies to this diagnostic stage; do not etch a tooth merely because a protocol card has an etch timer.
